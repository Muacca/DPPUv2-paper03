\section{Scalar derivatives and quadratic representation blocks}

\label{app:F}

For $x=(r,L,\eta,V)$, differentiating (S1) yields

\begin{equation}\tag{F1}\label{eq:F1}
\nabla U_0=\frac{\pi^2}{\kappa^2}
\begin{pmatrix}
L(3V^2r^2+9\eta^2-36)/12\\
r(V^2r^2+9\eta^2-36)/12\\
3Lr\eta/2\\ Lr^3V/6
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\tag{F2}\label{eq:F2}
H=\frac{\pi^2}{\kappa^2}
\begin{pmatrix}
LV^2r/2 &(3V^2r^2+9\eta^2-36)/12&3L\eta/2&LVr^2/2\\
(3V^2r^2+9\eta^2-36)/12&0&3r\eta/2&Vr^3/6\\
3L\eta/2&3r\eta/2&3Lr/2&0\\
LVr^2/2&Vr^3/6&0&Lr^3/6
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

At MX $(r,L,\eta,V,\kappa)=(3,1,1,1,1)$ the gradient is $\pi^2(0,-9/2,9/2,9/2)$; at AX $(3,1,1,0,1)$ it is $\pi^2(-9/4,-27/4,9/2,0)$. The nonzero gradients must accompany an interpretation of these matrices. In particular the AX $(r,\eta)$ determinant is negative, but the point is not a stationary saddle of the full scalar potential. Ordinary eigenvalues of a Hessian at such a point depend on the chosen coordinates and are not particle masses.

At $M=I,\omega=0$, the first shape derivative of (M3) is zero for every scalar coordinate. Differentiating it again with respect to any scalar proves all scalar--shape cross terms vanish. The twist contribution is even and quadratic in $\omega$, so its first derivative is zero there for every scalar and shape. Mixing does not enter the static scalar action. This directly gives the irreducible quadratic organization

\begin{equation}\tag{F3}\label{eq:F3}
(r,L,\eta,V)\ \oplus\ (\omega_i,\delta_i)\ \oplus\ (q_A),
\end{equation}

with scalar mixing retained inside the first block, (V1) in the second, and $8\mathcal K I_5$ in the third. This is an organization of the static quadratic form. It does not assume an independent kinetic term for every entry. In (S4) the bulk scalar velocity Hessian is $\mathrm{diag}(-3\pi^2r/(2\kappa^2L),0,0,0)$, consistent with the different roles of these coordinates.
