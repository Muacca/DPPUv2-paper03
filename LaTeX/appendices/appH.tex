\subsection*{H.2A.~Supplemental Scalar-Modulus Check with $L$ Released}

In the main text the $\Sone$ scale $L$ is fixed at $L=1$.  Here we temporarily
release $L$ and record supplemental results from a numerical evaluation of the
$4\times 4$ Hessian $H^{(r,L,\eta,V)}$ near the isotropic point.

\textit{Conditions:} $r_0=3$, $L_0=1$, $\kappa=1$, $\alpha=\theta_\NY=0$;
centred difference with $h=10^{-3}$ (stability confirmed at multiple step sizes).

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Background & $H_{LL}$ & $H_{rL}$ & $H_{L\eta}$ & $H_{LV}$ & $\lambda_\mathrm{min}$ \\
\midrule
AX & $\approx 0$ & $-177.65$ & $355.31$ & $0$      & $-349.79$ \\
VT & $\approx 0$ & $-59.22$  & $0$      & $355.31$ & $-360.20$ \\
MX & $\approx 0$ & $\approx 0$ & $355.31$ & $355.31$ & $-437.45$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{$4\times 4$ Hessian $H^{(r,L,\eta,V)}$ with $L$ released.  All cases
         are indefinite (saddle-point type).}
\end{table}

The full $4\times 4$ eigenvalues are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item AX: $-349.8,\; 130.1,\; 355.3,\; 575.0$
  \item VT: $-360.2,\; 121.4,\; 355.3,\; 712.6$
  \item MX: $-437.5,\; 23.7,\; 412.0,\; 830.9$
\end{itemize}
Since $H_{LL}\approx 0$, $L$ is a flat direction in the quadratic approximation
at the isotropic point, analogous to $r$.  The $L=1$ normalisation in the main
text is therefore appropriate.
Rather than a standard scalar degree of freedom with a stable mass, $L$ is a
modulus-type auxiliary variable that possesses large mixing couplings of the same
order as the diagonal scales ($|H_{rL}|=177.6$, $H_{L\eta}=355.3$).  Releasing
$L$ deepens the existing saddle-point structure: the minimum eigenvalue
deteriorates from $\lambda_\mathrm{min}=-35.9$ in the $(r,\eta)$ $2\times 2$
block to $\lambda_\mathrm{min}=-349.8$ in the full $4\times 4$ block.
Accordingly, $L$ is \emph{not} added to the final mode dictionary (H.3); the
treatment and potential stabilisation of $L$ is left as a separate analysis
beyond the scope of the present paper.

At the isotropic MX point the torsion scalar block satisfies numerically:
\begin{equation}
  H^{(\eta,V)} =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    355.31 & 0 \\
    0 & 355.31
  \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Before accounting for coupling to the radial mode, the torsion subsector is
locally stable and doubly degenerate.

\paragraph{$(r,\eta)$ coupled block eigenvalue analysis (AX mode, $\eta=1$, $V=0$):}

\begin{equation}
  H^{(r,\eta)} =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    H_{rr} & H_{r\eta} \\
    H_{\eta r} & H_{\eta\eta}
  \end{pmatrix}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \approx 0 & 118.44 \\
    118.44 & 355.31
  \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Quantity & Value \\
\midrule
$H_{rr}$     & $\approx 0$ ($\approx -10^{-6}$) \\
$H_{r\eta}$  & $118.435$ \\
$H_{\eta\eta}$ & $355.306$ \\
$\det H^{(r,\eta)}$ & $-1.40\times 10^4$ \\
$\lambda_1$ (minimum) & $-35.86$ \\
$\lambda_2$ (maximum) & $391.17$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Eigenvalue analysis of the $(r,\eta)$ coupled block in AX mode.}
\end{table}

$H_{rr}\approx 0$ indicates that $r$ is in a \emph{flat direction (zero mode)} of
the effective potential in the AX background.  Consequently $\det H^{(r,\eta)}<0$
and the $2\times 2$ block is saddle-point type (indefinite).  The eigenvector of
the negative eigenvalue is predominantly in the $r$ direction ($\approx -0.957\,
r + 0.290\,\eta$), so the instability originates in the flat direction of $r$ and
its coupling to $\eta$.  The eigenvalue of the predominantly $\eta$ direction
$\lambda_2\approx 391$ remains positive.

In the VT mode ($\eta=0$, $V=1$), $H_{rr}=118.44$ (non-zero) and $H_{r\eta}
\approx 0$, forming a positive-definite block.  Thus the $r$--$\eta$ mixing and
the $r$ zero mode are specific to the axial torsion component $\eta$.

\subsection{Composite Visualization at Selected $H_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}=0$ Points}

The following figures display, side by side, (i) the $(\varepsilon,\eta)$ phase
diagram, (ii) the effective potential $\Veff(r)$ at the selected point, and
(iii) the three spin-2 spectral branches $\{m=0,\,m=\pm 2,\,m=\pm 1\}$.  The
purpose is to provide a comprehensive view of the relationship between the
phase-diagram position, the potential shape, and the spectral splitting analysed
in \S\ref{sec:spin2} and Appendix~\ref{app:D}.

A note of caution: $H_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}=0$ is a \emph{local softening
condition along the squash direction} and does not always coincide with the
actual phase-transition point.

\paragraph{$\alpha=0$, Type-I representative point.}
Baseline softening figure.  Corresponds to the positive-side threshold
$\varepsilon_{c+}=0.48324$ of the main text, where the $m=0$ branch just reaches
zero while the $m=\pm 2$ branch remains massive.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig16_visualize_type-I.png}
  \caption{Composite visualization at a positive-$\varepsilon$, $\alpha=0$ point
           on the $H_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}=0$ locus.}
  \label{fig:vis_typeI}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{$\alpha<0$, Type-IW/IIW representative point.}
Example of local softening on the $\alpha<0$ side.  From paper~II, $\alpha\le 0$
is the stable side for the isotropic vacuum, forming the Type-IW and Type-IIW
stable phases.  The spectral splitting seen here is a normal branch splitting
associated with a shift of the background point, not a ghost instability.

The figures function as a supplemental phase atlas that allows simultaneous
comparison of the phase classification, the radial potential shape, and which
spectral branch participates in the softening.

\paragraph{$\alpha>0$, Type-G representative point.}
Supplemental visualisation is available on the ghost/metastable side, but the
physical interpretation of the spectral branches requires care.  In this paper
the region where $\alpha>0$ and the potential may possess a local well is
classified as Type-G in the phase diagram.  This region corresponds to the
ghost-unstable side, which does not yield a globally stable vacuum~\cite{Woodard2015}.
Accordingly, $H_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}=0$ and $H_{ss}=0$ within the Type-G
region should not be interpreted on the same footing as critical conditions in
the stable phases; a detailed physical interpretation of these loci is outside
the scope of the present paper.

\subsection{Remarks}

The numerical values in this appendix serve as reference anchors for the
stability discussions and mode dictionaries in the main text.  The phase atlas
here samples representative points only; full-grid supplemental data and
high-density figures for publication are deferred to subsequent work or
